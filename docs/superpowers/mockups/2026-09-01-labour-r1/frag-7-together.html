७ · How it all fits together

Everything above was one piece at a time. This last part puts them side by side, so you can judge the whole thing as one experience rather than seven screens. It ends with an honest list of what should be built now and what should wait — and one question that only you can answer.

1 · Four truths that must stay four

A farm day produces four different facts. They are recorded together, they are shown together, and they point at each other — but each one is true on its own, and each one can be known while the others are still unknown. Every time two of them are folded into one, the app starts claiming something nobody said.

What happened — the work log

Pruning on plot 2. Spraying at eleven at night because the power was on. The work itself, and when it operationally happened.

Fold it into attendance and a day where work was logged would quietly claim that people were there, and a day where people came but no work got done would erase them.

हजेरी — who was there

Names against days. Full, half, night, extra hours — or blank, meaning nobody has said yet.

Fold it into what was agreed and a man's presence would change the day you correct a rate. He either stood in that field or he did not; money has no vote in it.

What was agreed — रोजंदारी, or उक्ते

Either a rate per person per day, or one price for a whole job. Agreed before the work, spoken by you, never worked out by the app.

Fold it into acceptance and money looks settled the moment it was promised — a job would count as paid-for on the day it was described, before anyone had said it was finished.

Was it accepted — confirmed, or accepted as done

For a daily-wage week: the attendance was confirmed. For a job: the work was accepted, or it needs redoing.

Fold it into who-was-there and your record and the worker's agreement become the same single fact — so a disagreement has nothing left to disagree with, and the register stops being able to hold a dispute at all.

They connect: the work log gives attendance its headcount, attendance gives the money its evidence, and acceptance closes both. Connected, never merged.

2 · Two ways a week gets settled

Daily wage and contract work both end in someone saying "yes, that is right" — but they are asking different questions on the way there. Same farm memory, two different journeys.

रोजंदारी · daily wage

The question it keeps asking: who?

Work happened
How many came
Which of them, by name
The attendance is confirmed

Days are the thing being counted, so the days decide the money.

उक्ते · one price for the job

The question it keeps asking: how much is done?

The job and its price are agreed
Work runs across several days
How much of it got done
Accepted — or it needs redoing

Days are only evidence of effort. Finishing decides the money.

Note what is identical. Both loops record who came, on the same screen, in the same words, and both end in a human saying yes. Only the middle step differs. That is why one register can carry both — and why neither loop needs its own app.

3 · One week, everything on it at once

This is the frame worth the most of your time. It is a single ordinary week holding every hard case at once — two men on daily wage, a crew of eight on a fixed-price job, a night, a set of extra hours, and days nobody has spoken about yet. Nothing about it is a special screen. If this reads as one register and not as a system, the design holds.

One ordinary week: two people on daily wage, a crew of eight on a fixed-price job, a night mark, three extra hours, days nobody has spoken about — and no screen for any of it.

What to check in that frame, in order:

The crew reads as one line, not eight. शंकरसोबत 8 takes one line in the violet that already means मुकादम everywhere else in the app. Eight men did not turn the register into a wall of names.

The dash on the crew's money is the honest one. You owe शंकर ₹12,000. What he pays each of his eight is between them, so the app says so instead of inventing a share. The ₹12,000 is written once, against the job, underneath.

The night and the extra hours did not need new cells. The same box splits: the day fact on top, the second fact under it. No clock, no start time — 3 तास means three hours long, never three o'clock.

Two boxes are simply empty. Nobody has said anything about रमेश on Friday or सुनीता on Thursday. An empty box is not a नाही, and Friday's headcount honestly reads 2 — the two people known to have come, not a claim about the rest.

The bottom-right day box is blank on purpose. Three people's days and one crew's days are not the same unit, and adding them would state a number nobody said. Whether a crew line should end in days, in people, or in nothing is still your open question from the first pack.

The week's money is ₹5,800, not ₹17,800. It adds up only the wages you actually stated. The job's ₹12,000 is not week money — it falls due when you accept the work, which is why it lives on the job and not in the week's line.

Fair warning about this frame: it is drawn the recommended way from the money brief — the crew's money reads as a dash and the agreed amount sits on the job. If you decide differently, this frame changes and so does the register. That is the one question at the end of this page.

4 · What the farmer is still doing

All of the above is machinery. None of it may reach him. His head holds four questions, and it must still hold exactly four when this ships:

किती माणसं आली?
काय काम झालं?
₹ किती पैसा लागला/ठरला?
काम वेळेत आणि नीट झालं का?

He must never feel he has opened something for managing contracts. He thinks "हे काम मी शंकरला उक्ते दिलंय" — he told the app once, in his own words, and the app remembers the rest. He never picks an arrangement from a list, he never sets anything up, and he is never asked again for something he has already said.

That is still the test: where is ShramSafal asking me for something I already told it?

5 · What to build now, and what can wait

Not everything in this document should be built. Some of it must be decided now only because deciding it later would mean tearing up what is being built. The rest can arrive whenever it earns its place, and nothing already drawn will have to change to receive it.

The idea Now or later Why
Recording who came, the ladder from a stranger to a known man, the register, and the one switch that lets a मुकादम take attendance NOW This is the release in front of you. It is fully drawn, it stands on its own, and nothing below is needed for it to work.
Remembering whether hours were said out loud or worked out by the app NOWone small thing The one item on this list that cannot be recovered afterwards. Once a week is saved without it, nobody can ever tell again which of those hours a man actually spoke and which the app worked out for him. It is small today and impossible later.
Which arrangement a job was agreed under, and what the money at the end of a contract line means DECIDE NOW It changes the register, and the register is being built this month. The register has never held a single real day of farm data, so changing it today is free and changing it after the pilot means rebuilding it under live records.
The one-tap timer — start, break, stop LATER It only adds a new fact. Everything drawn keeps working whether or not a timer ever exists, and a week recorded without one loses nothing.
Farm shift templates — what a normal working day is on this farm LATERand after the timer A normal day means nothing until there is a real measured day to hold it against. Built before the timer it would just be a setting nobody uses.
The usual rate for a kind of work, remembered per farm LATER A convenience — it saves saying a number you say often. It changes nothing about what is true, and the app must still print only what you actually said.
A मुकादम running his contracts across several farms LATERdifferent product A different person with a different job, who would need his own screens and his own money view. Worth naming out loud precisely so it stops bending this release, which is built for the farm owner.

Nothing on this page changes anything you have already approved — except one thing: what the money at the end of a contract line means. That is the single question that needs your answer before the register gets built.
