६ · New idea — रोजंदारी and उक्ते काम

This is the bigger of the two new ideas. Everything so far has assumed one way of paying for work: so many people, so many days, so much per person. That is only half of what happens on a real farm. The other half is a whole job agreed for one price before anybody starts. The register has to hold both — and hold them honestly.

रोजंदारी

daily wage

Paid per person, per day. Six people worked a full day at ₹500 each. Another day worked means more money owed.

Attendance and money are tightly linked. Counting the days is counting the money.

उक्ते काम

contract / piece work

A whole job agreed for a fixed amount, before it starts. Prune these 2 acres for ₹12,000. Whether the crew takes one day or three, the farmer owes ₹12,000.

Attendance is evidence of effort here — but it is not the basis of payment.

The one thing to hold on to

These are not two kinds of worker. They are two ways of agreeing how a job is paid for.

The same शंकर can be on daily wage on Monday and take a contract on Wednesday. Nothing about him changed in between — only the arrangement for that one piece of work. So the arrangement belongs to the work, and never permanently to the person. The app must never stamp a man as a contract man.

One man, one week

श शंकर सोमवार · रोजंदारी बुधवार · उक्ते काम

Both days are true, both are his, and they are paid by completely different rules. If the app works out the money rule from who the person is, one of these two days will be wrong every single time.

What a contract has to say — the smallest honest card

Seven things, and no more. What work · where · given to whom · how much work · measured how · the agreed amount · by when. Every one of them is something the farmer said out loud when he struck the deal. Nothing on this card is worked out by the app.

The collision — what goes in a worker's money column?

Under this contract the farmer owes शंकर ₹12,000. What शंकर pays each of his eight people is a private arrangement between them — it might be ₹1,400, it might be nothing this week, and he keeps a margin for organising, transport and risk. The farmer does not know that number, so the app does not know it either.

✗ never do this

गणेश ₹1,500

₹12,000 ÷ 8 = ₹1,500. Nobody ever said that number. It is the app splitting a contract into wages it has no knowledge of — and printing it inside a document meant to be proof of payment.

✓ the honest render

गणेश —

His attendance is fully known and fully recorded. His pay is not the farmer's to state. The dash already means nobody said. The ₹12,000 is shown once, against the job.

The case that rules out every easy answer

It is tempting to say contract weeks simply get a different screen. They cannot, because both arrangements can run on the same plot, on the same day:

तण काढणे · weeding

4 workers · रोजंदारी · ₹450 each, stated by the farmer

छाटणी · pruning

शंकर's 8 · उक्ते काम · ₹12,000 for the job

Same farm. Same plot. Same date. So neither the farm, nor the plot, nor the day, nor the person can decide which money rule applies — the job decides it. Which means one register has to hold rows of both kinds at once, and the money column can mean two different things on rows sitting right next to each other.

Attendance still matters under a contract

It would be easy to think that once the price is fixed, who turned up stops mattering. It does not. The farmer still wants to know that 8 came Monday, 6 Tuesday and 4 Wednesday — because that is the only way anyone ever learns how much human effort this kind of job really takes.

After a few seasons the app can say something no paper book ever could: pruning two acres has taken between 16 and 22 person-days every time. That is how a farmer finds out whether the next price he is quoted is fair. Recording attendance under contract work is not paperwork — it is the farm building its own memory.

One warning, stated plainly

What the farmer agreed with the मुकादम, and what the मुकादम pays his own workers, are two different commercial facts. They belong to two different sets of people.

The app must never treat one as the other, and must never quietly show one person's money to another. A register that is safe to hand around the farm becomes unsafe the moment a private number leaks into it — and the leak would not look like a mistake, it would look like a fact.

This idea is already half-built

Worth saying, because it changes how big this looks: the app is not starting from nothing here.

already there today

  • It can already record that a job was agreed by acre, by tree, by row, or as one lump sum.
  • It already keeps whether a price was per person or a whole-job amount.
  • It already knows a piece of work can be daily wage, contract, or done by the farmer himself.

genuinely missing

  • A standing agreement that several days of work can point back to. Today each day stands on its own, so nothing holds the deal together across the week.
  • A way to confirm the job was finished and accepted — the moment a farmer actually settles a contract in real life.

So the question in front of you is not whether to build contract work from scratch. It is whether the pieces already in the app should be joined into one agreement the farmer can see, add days to, and close.
