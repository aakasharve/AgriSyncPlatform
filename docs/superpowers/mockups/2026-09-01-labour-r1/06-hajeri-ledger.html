The हजेरी वही — five realities, Mukadam-wise, three views

The built ledger is the baseline, not a starting point. Every cell, colour, size and column below is lifted verbatim from HajeriLedger.tsx / LabourUiKit.tsx. This screen extends the grid — it does not restyle it.

The test every frame here must pass

“Where is ShramSafal asking me for something I already told it?”

Answer for this screen: the वही only reads back. It asks for exactly two things, and only where a fact is genuinely missing — who the stated headcount was (he gave a count, never the names), and what happened on a day he himself declared no work. It never re-asks a plot, a crop, a count or a name he already said.

Two fidelity notes, so nothing here is oversold. (1) At exactly 390px the shipped grid is ~14px wider than its card, so the last day column sits under the card's own overflow-x-auto — that is the built behaviour at this width, reproduced, not a mockup bug (it fits a 412px phone). Where money adds a column, the same card is repeated unrolled below the phone so you can see it without scrolling. (2) The shipped body padding is pb-24 (tab-bar space); trimmed here only so 13 frames stack readably.

Panel 1 · The वही is always there

Four conditions in which a farmer can open हजेरी वही. In every one the grid is present — day columns and a name column — because a register with nothing written in it is still a register. An unknown day is blank; – is reserved for “he did not come”.

Panel 2 · All five realities, inside the 26px cell

This is the new part and it needs your eye. Hours are a duration, never a clock — there is no 7:00–11:00 anywhere, no start/end field, no time picker. The cell splits: the day fact on top, the second fact below.

Panel 3 · Mukadam-wise — your exact day

Ganesh runs your farm. Shankar brings a crew. Ramesh and Sunita you hired yourself. Every one of them owns his own presence; the crew is a separate line under Shankar, never folded into his cell.

Panel 4 · Money, only where money was spoken

Normal days carry attendance marks and nothing else. A day paid off the standard rate carries that amount in that day's cell. A row ends in an amount only where that single amount was stated for that person, and the bottom line stays a dash unless he stated a week figure himself. Every ₹ here is a number the farmer said — the वही never multiplies days by a rate and never adds two amounts together.

Already rejected — kept here so it stays rejected

An amount in every cell. At 26px an amount can only be drawn at ~8.5px, which is smaller than the text telling him which money it is:

Why the एकूण money reads — and not ₹750. A week total is drawn only when the farmer stated a week total himself. Here he stated ₹450 on one day and ₹300 on another and never stated a week figure, so the bottom line is the house mark for a fact nobody supplied. Adding two stated amounts together would be ShramSafal calculating money, and ShramSafal never calculates money — it only shows back what he said. The domain holds the same line in code: LabourAssignment.cs:46, the NO-MULTIPLY honesty rule, keeps TotalCost stored as-given, never computed.

Panel 5 · The same week, three views

Owner, authorised Mukadam, worker. There is no fourth. A suspended or not-yet-approved member is not a view — this surface is never drawn for them at all.

PANEL 6 — AWAITING FOUNDER APPROVAL. This is a question, not a decision.

The fourth axis — where does worker confirmation live?

The cell already carries three things: how much (full / half / hours), day-or-night, and money on an off-rate day. Worker confirmation would be a fourth. The spec's warning is the whole reason this is a question: adding a fourth axis after the grid ships means rebuilding the grid.

The proposed resolution, drawn below for you to rule on: confirmation lives on the row — one line per person for the whole week — and a disputed day is the single exception that is allowed to touch a cell. Nothing else ever enters the cell.

Your ruling needed on three things

  1. Confirmation on the row, not the cell — yes or no. If it must be per-day, the grid has to be rebuilt before it ships, not after.
  2. A disputed day being the one cell-level exception — yes or no.
  3. Whether an unconfirmed week is allowed to look identical to a confirmed one until the worker acts (drawn here as a plain grey line, so silence never reads as agreement).
