Can the हजेरी register still be the wage book?

One decision is blocking a UI approval. This file explains it from zero — you need no prior context, no access to the codebase, and no knowledge of earlier design decisions. Everything needed is on this page.

The short version: a design decision called D-H6 said the attendance register and the wage book are the same book. That was decided while thinking only about daily-wage labour. A second kind of farm work — fixed-price contract work — has now entered the picture, and under it the app cannot know what any individual worker earned. So the money column would have to print a number nobody knows. This page asks how to resolve that.

1 · The words you need

This is an app for smallholder farmers in Maharashtra, India. The interface is in Marathi.

हजेरी (hajeri) — attendance
Who physically turned up to work on a given day. The हजेरी वही (hajeri vahi) is the muster register — the paper book a farm keeps, with names down the side and days across the top.
मुकादम (mukadam) — labour organiser / foreman
A person who brings a group of workers to a farm. He may work himself, or only organise. The farmer often deals with him, not with each worker. He may bring 8 people to one farm on Monday and 4 to another on Tuesday.
रोजंदारी (rojandari) — daily wage
Paid per person per day. 6 workers × a full day × ₹500 each. More days worked means more money owed. Attendance and money are tightly linked.
उक्ते काम (ukte kaam) — contract / piece work
A whole job agreed for a fixed amount, before it starts. “Prune these 2 acres for ₹12,000.” Whether the crew finishes in one day or three, the farmer owes ₹12,000. Attendance is evidence of effort, but it is not the basis of payment.
Critical point, already settled: these are not two kinds of worker. They are two ways of agreeing how a job will be paid for. The same Shankar can work on daily wage on Monday and take a contract on Wednesday — and both can happen on the same farm, the same plot, on the same day.

2 · The three prior decisions this touches

Quoted from the design spec. “D-H” = हजेरी Design decision.

D-H6 — The register IS the wage book

“Asked: on the farms he knows, is the हजेरी book the same book as the payment record, or two different books? Answered: the same book. Consequence, which is larger than the layout: the PDF is a proof of payment, not a proof of attendance.”

This is the decision now in question.

D-H7 — Money appears only where the day was unusual

“Normal days show only the attendance marks. A day paid differently from the standard rate shows its amount. Every row ends in the week's money, and so does the bottom line. Rejected: an amount in every cell — it renders at 8.5px, smaller than anything else in the app, on the screen a man reads in sunlight to check his own pay.”

D-H8 — Same book, different pages

“An attendance register is safe to show anyone on the farm. A wage book is not. The moment money enters the grid, showing the हजेरी वही to a मुकादम shows him what every man in his crew earns.” Resolved as three views — Owner sees the whole book; मुकादम sees his crew's attendance but no wage roster; Worker sees his own row only.

One more rule, and it is absolute: the app displays money the farmer stated and never calculates one. No rate × days. No derived totals. This is enforced in the code itself — the amount field is documented as stored as-given, never computed. A number the app invents is the one thing this product may not do.

3 · How to read the register

Names down the side, seven days across, one cell per person per day. Four cell states:

आला · came ½अर्धा · half day –नाही · did not come blank · nobody said

The blank cell matters more than it looks. A dashed empty cell means nobody has told the app anything about that day. The grey – means someone said he did not come. Those are different facts and the app must never confuse them — an absence of information is not evidence of absence.

4 · The design as approved — daily wage

This works. Six people, paid per day. Money sits at the end of each row, exactly as D-H7 says. Tuesday was paid off the standard rate, so that day carries its own amount. Every figure here is a number the farmer said out loud.

२४–३० ऑग · हजेरी वही
दिवस र सो मं बु गु ₹
गगणेश ½ ₹1,800

हिरवा = आला · पिवळा = अर्धा दिवस · राखाडी = नाही

Daily wage. Days → money. The register really is the wage book here.

5 · The collision — the same register, contract work

Now Shankar takes the pruning of Plot 2 for a fixed ₹12,000. He brings eight people. They work Monday to Thursday. The farmer records who came, as always.

Now ask: what goes in Ganesh's money column?
The farmer owes Shankar ₹12,000. What Shankar pays Ganesh is a private arrangement between them — it might be ₹1,400, it might be nothing this week, and Shankar keeps a margin for organising, transport and risk. The farmer does not know this number, so the app does not know it either.

✗ What we must never do

गणेश ₹1,500

₹12,000 ÷ 8 people = ₹1,500. An invented number. Nobody said it. It would be the app splitting a contract into wages it has no knowledge of — and it would appear in a document described as proof of payment.

✓ The honest render

गणेश —

His attendance is fully known and fully recorded. His pay is not the farmer's to state. The dash already means nobody said — the register's existing word for an unknown. The ₹12,000 is shown once, against the job.

6 · The case that rules out the easy answers

It is tempting to say “contract weeks get a different screen.” But both arrangements can run on the same plot, on the same day:

तण काढणे · weeding

4 workers · रोजंदारी · ₹450 each, stated by the farmer

छाटणी · pruning

Shankar's 8 · उक्ते काम · ₹12,000 for the job

Same farm. Same plot. Same date. So neither the farm, nor the plot, nor the day, nor the person can decide which money rule applies. It is decided by the job — which means one register can contain rows of both kinds at once, and the money column may mean different things on adjacent rows.

7 · The decision

When Shankar's crew works उक्ते and you open the हजेरी for that week — what do you expect to see against each of those eight names?

Why this is being asked now and not later: the attendance table has never received a single row in production. Changing what the money column means is free today and expensive the moment real farm data exists. A closely-related decision earlier in this project carried the warning “adding a fourth axis after the grid ships means rebuilding it.” This is the same shape of decision.

8 · Decision record

To be completed after the founder decides. This section becomes the permanent visual reference for why the register looks the way it does.

Chosen option
A FOURTH WAY, better than all three offered: the register goes fully clean — no money in any cell, row or total, and no totals column at all. Money lives on the Labour home as two separate cards (रोजंदारी नोंदलेली / उक्ते ठरलेली, never combined) and in tap-detail.
Decided on
2 September 2026, via the founder's integrated master review file.
Effect on D-H6
D-H7 (money in unusual-day cells + row-end money) is SUPERSEDED. D-H6's intent survives one level up: attendance and money remain one Labour surface, reached by tap — but the grid itself is purely attendance. The mixed-week problem disappears entirely.
Screens affected
Register (06) — money and totals column removed, उक्ते dot added; Labour home — two money cards added; tap-detail — new.
