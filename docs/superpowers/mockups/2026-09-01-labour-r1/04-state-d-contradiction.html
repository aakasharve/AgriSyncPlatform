State D — the contradiction

Two kinds of contradiction, both drawn. A — the headcount disagreement: the Work Log said 12, speaking the names produced 10, and both statements survive. B — the day-level contradiction: one person implies Full in one work and Half in another on the same farm-day, so ShramSafal asks one polite question, once, and then stops. The last frame is the case that asks nothing.

The framing that decides this screen: this is a semantic question asked before saving — ShramSafal noticing that two things it was told cannot both be a whole day, and asking. It is not a database error surfaced to the farmer. Nothing below may look like a failure, a crash, a validation error or a red alert.
The test every frame must pass: "Where is ShramSafal asking me for something I already told it?" Each caption states what the farmer already told it, and what — the single unstated thing — is asked here.

The Work Log's "12" and the ten names that were actually spoken sit side by side with neither one overwritten, and the owner is asked the single thing nobody has stated: whether the other two were there.

Already told it: the count (12), the ten names, the work, the plot, the day. Asked here: only what was never said — who or what the remaining two are.

Reading notes

  • Marathi shown verbatim (already approved, not reworded): एक गोष्ट स्पष्ट करा · ShramSafalला समजलं · 12 पैकी 10 समजले · बरोबर · बदल करा · पूर्ण · अर्धा · हजेरी वही · दिवस · मागे. Everything else inside a frame is a placeholder chip with its English meaning underneath.
  • "12 पैकी 10 समजले" reuses the approved sentence with this day's own numbers. The words are unchanged; only the figures — which are data, not copy — differ from the approved example.
  • द्राक्ष · ऊस · the ten names are farm data read back from what the farmer already logged, not new copy. Worker names are the product, not something to scrub.
  • No clock anywhere. हजेरी remembers duration — पूर्ण · अर्धा · रात्र · जादा 2 तास · 4 तास — never a start/end time. Operational timing belongs to the Work Log. No frame shows a derived half-day number and no frame shows a wage.
  • Frames 1 → 2 are one sequence (ask, then resolved). Frames 3 → 4 are the other (ask, then it stops). Frame 5 stands alone: it is the same day shape with nothing asked.

What this screen must never do

Check the rule as well as the picture — each line is a constraint the frames above are meant to satisfy.

  • ✕Never an error state. No "something went wrong", no failure framing, no retry banner. This is a question asked before saving, not a fault report.
  • ✕Never red or destructive styling. No rose error card, no red border, no warning triangle in front of the farmer. Green explain-card, plain white options.
  • ✕Never a stack trace, an error code, or any code. Nothing technical reaches this surface.
  • ✕Never a reconciliation dashboard. No list of open conflicts, no count of disagreements, no queue to work through.
  • ✕Never a per-person-per-task timesheet. The two work cards report what each work said; they are not a grid of people × tasks × hours to fill in.
  • ✕Never more than one question. One question per contradiction, asked once, then it stops — and none at all when the statements agree.
  • ✕Never deduplicate anonymous crew. Two unnamed people stay two unnamed people: never merged into one, never matched to a known worker, never given a guessed name.
  • ✕Never silently overwrite a statement. The 12 stays 12 and the अर्धा stays अर्धा. An answer is recorded beside what was said, never in place of it.
  • ✕Never re-ask what was already told. No plot, no crop, no count, no name is asked for again — only the one thing that was never stated.
  • ✕Never a clock range, a derived 0.5/1.5 day, or a wage. Durations are stated facts and stay stated.
  • ✕Never render an unknown day as "–". A blank dashed cell means nobody said; the grey dash means he did not come. They are different facts.
