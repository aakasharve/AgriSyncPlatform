Labour Management — what we have, and the new ideas

This document has two parts. Part one is the Labour Management system as it already is — the capture loop, the ladder, the हजेरी वही, and the one switch that says who is allowed to write in it. Part two is two new ideas that are not part of that release, drawn against those same screens to show how each would fold into what part one already does — and what, if anything, it would change.

१ · Recording who came — the capture journey

This is one journey, not four screens. The farmer speaks his day into the main log the way he always has. The app understands from that sentence that 12 people worked on द्राक्ष-२ today. Labour then asks him the one thing that sentence did not contain — who those twelve were. He answers out loud. ShramSafal shows him its own answer, he taps बरोबर, and the day is recorded. That is the entire loop.

Everything below is drawn, not running. The names, dates and numbers are sample content.

The loop, in five steps

What to check across all six screens

Count the questions. Across the whole journey the app asks exactly one thing — या 12 जणांमध्ये कोण होते? — and, only when two of his own statements disagree, one more to settle a single person's day. The date, the plot, the crop, the headcount and every name he already spoke are printed back to him as facts, never as fields. There is no plot chooser, no counter, no clock, no form, and no amount of money anywhere in this journey.

३ · The हजेरी वही — the register that remembers

This is the book the farm already keeps, drawn on a phone. It does not interview the farmer and it does not do sums for him. It reads back what was said — and it is careful about the difference between something that was said and something nobody has said yet. Everything below sits in the ledger that is already built and already liked: same box, same colours, same size. What is new is what that box is now allowed to hold.

Against the test on this page, the हजेरी वही answers nowhere — it only shows back. It never re-asks a plot, a crop, a count or a name that was already given.

Not knowing a man was away is not the same as knowing he was away. Putting a dash where nobody spoke would be ShramSafal inventing an absence, and that invented absence is the one thing this release exists to remove. So an empty box stays empty, and a week nobody spoke about ends in —, never 0.

3.2  The register is never blocked

A book with nothing written in it is still a book. The हजेरी वही opens with no work written down for the day, with no headcount given, with not a single mark on the page, with nobody added to the farm yet, and on a day the farmer himself said no work happened. In every one of those the week is drawn and the days are drawn — because the page has to be there for him to write into.

Where money sits today, and what it is allowed to do

  • •  An ordinary day carries attendance marks and nothing else.
  • •  A day paid differently from the usual rate carries that amount inside that day.
  • •  The end of a person’s row carries the week’s money — only where a figure was actually stated for that person.
  • •  Where nothing was said about money, the row ends in —, never ₹0. Zero is a claim; a dash is honest.

Every rupee shown is a rupee the farmer said. ShramSafal counts marks; it never works out money. It does not multiply days by a rate, and it does not add two stated amounts together — which is why the bottom money line is a dash even though ₹4,650 and ₹450 are both on the page. No week figure was ever spoken, so there isn’t one.

Your ruling for the day this arrives

Every मुकादम you already have starts OFF. Nobody quietly keeps what he had. You say yes, one man at a time.

रोकडे OFF धनाजी OFF रमेश OFF प्रकाश OFF

Nothing about who works for you changes — all four are still मुकादम. Only one thing is new, and it is yours to say out loud.

What this must never turn into

  • Never a grid of people against things they may do. One man, one line, one switch.
  • Never three switches for attendance, money and approvals. There is one thing to allow, not three.
  • Never a separate screen for “until Sunday”. The day it ends belongs inside the same switch.
  • Never make you approve each day. ON means he gets on with it — you keep the book, not a queue of permissions to grant.

The honest verdict on this one

The timer can wait. One piece inside it cannot.

The timer itself is safe to build later, and I would rather it came later. It only adds a new fact. Adding it in six months costs six months of sessions that were never recorded, and a session that was never recorded is honestly missing.

The one piece that cannot wait

Hours are already being recorded — 4 तास, जादा 2 तास. Every one of those hours has to carry, from the very first day it is written down, whether a person said it or a clock counted it. Right now every hour is a said hour, so the mark costs nothing to add and changes nothing on screen.

Add that mark after a season of hours has been recorded, and every one of those earlier hours becomes a number nobody can place. Was that eight spoken by the farmer, or counted by a phone? There is no way to go back and ask. It is the same failure as a blank square being printed as –: not a wrong number, an unrecoverable one.

Against your own test — "where is ShramSafal asking me for something I already told it?" — this idea asks for nothing. It has no fields to fill. It records presses, and it can be ignored entirely.

६ · New idea — रोजंदारी and उक्ते काम

This is the bigger of the two new ideas. Everything so far has assumed one way of paying for work: so many people, so many days, so much per person. That is only half of what happens on a real farm. The other half is a whole job agreed for one price before anybody starts. The register has to hold both — and hold them honestly.

रोजंदारी

daily wage

Paid per person, per day. Six people worked a full day at ₹500 each. Another day worked means more money owed.

Attendance and money are tightly linked. Counting the days is counting the money.

उक्ते काम

contract / piece work

A whole job agreed for a fixed amount, before it starts. Prune these 2 acres for ₹12,000. Whether the crew takes one day or three, the farmer owes ₹12,000.

Attendance is evidence of effort here — but it is not the basis of payment.

The one thing to hold on to

These are not two kinds of worker. They are two ways of agreeing how a job is paid for.

The same शंकर can be on daily wage on Monday and take a contract on Wednesday. Nothing about him changed in between — only the arrangement for that one piece of work. So the arrangement belongs to the work, and never permanently to the person. The app must never stamp a man as a contract man.

One man, one week

Both days are true, both are his, and they are paid by completely different rules. If the app works out the money rule from who the person is, one of these two days will be wrong every single time.

Attendance still matters under a contract

It would be easy to think that once the price is fixed, who turned up stops mattering. It does not. The farmer still wants to know that 8 came Monday, 6 Tuesday and 4 Wednesday — because that is the only way anyone ever learns how much human effort this kind of job really takes.

After a few seasons the app can say something no paper book ever could: pruning two acres has taken between 16 and 22 person-days every time. That is how a farmer finds out whether the next price he is quoted is fair. Recording attendance under contract work is not paperwork — it is the farm building its own memory.

One warning, stated plainly

What the farmer agreed with the मुकादम, and what the मुकादम pays his own workers, are two different commercial facts. They belong to two different sets of people.

The app must never treat one as the other, and must never quietly show one person's money to another. A register that is safe to hand around the farm becomes unsafe the moment a private number leaks into it — and the leak would not look like a mistake, it would look like a fact.

This idea is already half-built

७ · How it all fits together

Everything above was one piece at a time. This last part puts them side by side, so you can judge the whole thing as one experience rather than seven screens. It ends with an honest list of what should be built now and what should wait — and one question that only you can answer.

1 · Four truths that must stay four

A farm day produces four different facts. They are recorded together, they are shown together, and they point at each other — but each one is true on its own, and each one can be known while the others are still unknown. Every time two of them are folded into one, the app starts claiming something nobody said.

What happened — the work log

Pruning on plot 2. Spraying at eleven at night because the power was on. The work itself, and when it operationally happened.

Fold it into attendance and a day where work was logged would quietly claim that people were there, and a day where people came but no work got done would erase them.

हजेरी — who was there

Names against days. Full, half, night, extra hours — or blank, meaning nobody has said yet.

Fold it into what was agreed and a man's presence would change the day you correct a rate. He either stood in that field or he did not; money has no vote in it.

What was agreed — रोजंदारी, or उक्ते

Either a rate per person per day, or one price for a whole job. Agreed before the work, spoken by you, never worked out by the app.

Fold it into acceptance and money looks settled the moment it was promised — a job would count as paid-for on the day it was described, before anyone had said it was finished.

Was it accepted — confirmed, or accepted as done

For a daily-wage week: the attendance was confirmed. For a job: the work was accepted, or it needs redoing.

Fold it into who-was-there and your record and the worker's agreement become the same single fact — so a disagreement has nothing left to disagree with, and the register stops being able to hold a dispute at all.

They connect: the work log gives attendance its headcount, attendance gives the money its evidence, and acceptance closes both. Connected, never merged.

Before any of this gets built

One open question: what does the money column mean on a contract row?

Everything else on this page either already holds or can wait without costing anything. This one cannot, because it changes the shape of the register and the register is what gets built next. On a रोजंदारी row the money column is plain — it is the wage you stated for that person. On an उक्ते काम row there is no per-person wage to state: you agreed one amount for the whole job, and what the मुकादम pays each of his people is between them. There are three ways to answer it, and only three.

Option A · recommended

A dash in the row, with the agreed amount shown once against the job

The contract row's money column reads —, which already means nobody said everywhere else in the register. The ₹12,000 sits once on the job card underneath. Nothing is invented, no private number leaks, and the column keeps a single meaning: money that was actually spoken for this person.

This is how sections 6 and 7 above are drawn.

Option B

The money column appears only where wages exist

A contract band carries no money column at all — not a dash, nothing. The column is drawn only over the रोजंदारी rows. Honest, and it removes the dash entirely; but one page then has two different row shapes, and the eye has to work out which kind of row it is looking at before it can read across.

Option C

Money leaves the register entirely

The हजेरी वही goes back to being purely a record of presence, and everything owed lives beside it on its own surface. The cleanest rule of the three and the safest to hand around the farm — but it takes money off the page where you are most likely to want to see it, and it is the largest change to what is already drawn.

Nothing else needs a decision to proceed. Part one is settled and can be built as drawn. The timer in section 5 can wait — with the single exception named there: an hour must record, from the first day it is written down, whether a person said it or a clock counted it. Answer the money question above and the register can be built.
