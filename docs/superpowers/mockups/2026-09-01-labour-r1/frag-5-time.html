५ · New idea — when the work actually happened

Part two begins — this is a new idea, not approved work

Everything before this point is the release you have already walked through. From here the question changes: is this idea worth building at all, and when? Nothing below has been designed into the release and nothing has been built. If you say no to all of it, nothing above this line moves.

What the register still cannot answer

हजेरी answers one question, and answers it well: who came. It does not answer for how long, during which part of the day, or with what interruptions in between. And on a farm the day is rarely one clean block. The electricity window decides when the pump runs. Irrigation stops so spraying can happen. Harvesting runs past dark. A night operation belongs to a date the calendar argues with.

Three days can all read पूर्ण and have been three completely different days of work. The register is not wrong in any of them — it was only ever asked who came.

The idea — four presses, nothing typed

One timer, operated by the owner or by a मुकादम he has already authorised. It is pressed, not filled in. Four presses across a day are enough to hold the shape of that day.

2 · The timer belongs to the work, not to the farm

One farm is not one clock. Eight people can be pruning grapes from first light while four others irrigate cane in the afternoon, and the pump may only run at night when the three-phase power is on. A single farm-wide timer would be a lie about all three at once. So a timer is attached to a job, and a farm can have several running side by side.

The rules this idea is not allowed to break

A timer is the easiest thing in this whole document to build and the easiest to get quietly wrong. Four things have to stay visible on the screen, not merely true underneath it.

1 The timer is not attendance

"Work ran from eight to five" is a fact about the work. It says nothing about who stood in that field, and it must never be allowed to fill in anybody's row. The two sit on one screen as two separate cards, and the session card never grows a tick.

2 Who pressed the button is not who worked

The same rule the rest of the app already lives by. शंकर pressing the button at eight in the morning is a fact about his phone, not about his hands. He appears on the session as the man who ran it, in violet, and his own row in the register is untouched by that — which is exactly what the frame above shows: the man who ran the timer has a blank square.

श शंकर pressed the button ≠ worked the day

3 A counted hour and a spoken hour must never look the same

This is the subtlest part of the idea and the part most likely to be lost. If the timer runs eight to twelve and one to five, the app may honestly show eight hours. A farmer may also simply say "8 तास". Both are true. They are not the same kind of true, and the screen has to say so without needing a word of explanation.

The two hour-marks, magnified

8 तास

Said by a person

Solid block, a microphone, and his own round number in his own word. There are no clock times sitting behind it, so none are shown and none are ever invented.

8:00 08:00–12:00 · 13:00–17:00

Counted by the phone

Outlined block, a clock, minutes always shown, and the presses it was counted from printed underneath it. It is never allowed to appear on its own.

The test. Cover the English words with your thumb and look only at the two blocks. They are still telling you two different things — one is filled and one is outlined, one carries a mouth and one carries a clock, one is a round number and one carries its own working underneath it. That is the whole rule.

4 Optional, and always correctable

Farms forget to press buttons. Work starts while the phone is still in a pocket. If the only way to have honest hours is to remember a button at the exact moment work begins, the hours will be dishonest within a week. So a day with no timer is a perfectly normal day, and a late press is a thing you fix in one spoken line — with the phone's own reading kept underneath it, never erased.

The honest verdict on this one

The timer can wait. One piece inside it cannot.

The timer itself is safe to build later, and I would rather it came later. It only adds a new fact. It does not disturb a single thing already designed — the register keeps its squares, the voice flow keeps its questions, and nobody is asked anything new. A day without a timer simply has no timer. Adding it in six months costs six months of sessions that were never recorded, and a session that was never recorded is honestly missing.

The one piece that cannot wait

Hours are already being recorded — 4 तास, जादा 2 तास. Every one of those hours has to carry, from the very first day it is written down, whether a person said it or a clock counted it. Right now every hour is a said hour, so the mark costs nothing to add and changes nothing on screen.

Add that mark after a season of hours has been recorded, and every one of those earlier hours becomes a number nobody can place. Was that eight spoken by the farmer, or counted by a phone? There is no way to go back and ask. It is the same failure as a blank square being printed as –: not a wrong number, an unrecoverable one.

Against your own test — "where is ShramSafal asking me for something I already told it?" — this idea asks for nothing. It has no fields to fill. It records presses, and it can be ignored entirely.
