## 1. The one-liner

**Speak for one minute. ShramSafal turns your day's farm work into clear records.**

A voice-first farm operations app for smallholder Indian farmers. The farmer talks — in their own spoken Marathi — about what happened on the farm today. ShramSafal listens, understands, and silently sorts it into a structured record: work done, cost, labour, water, inputs, machinery, and what's still pending. No typing. No forms. No app-literacy required.

**Category we're creating:** the *daily farm work companion* — not an "agri-app," not a marketplace, not an advisory bot. The thing a farmer opens every evening the way a shopkeeper closes the till.

---

## 2. The problem (the emotional hook for all creative)

> *"The problem is not hard work. The problem is scattered work."*

The smallholder farmer is, in business terms, a CEO, CFO, HR head, and operations manager — running a high-risk outdoor business with no record system. By nightfall, half of today is already mixed up in memory.

The four pains (use these verbatim — they test well):
- **Work happened, but the record is weak.**
- **Money was spent, but the reason is unclear.**
- **Labour came, but no one knows the real output.**
- **Tasks are pending, but there's no single clear picture.**

And the cost of that gap is silent: it quietly eats **money, time, and control.** That's the wound. ShramSafal is the one-minute-a-day habit that closes it.

**The shift we sell:** from *"I think"* → *"I know."* From a farm that runs on memory, phone calls, and guesswork → a farm that runs on real information.

## 4. The brand promise & narrative arc

The marketing narrative runs as a 9-chapter scroll story on the site. The agency should treat this as the canonical emotional spine:

1. **The Field** — where the farmer lives.
2. **The Pain** — scattered work.
3. **The Shift** — same farm, very different control.
4. **Speak and We Sort** — the magic moment (the product demo).
5. **The 90-Day Return** — value builds step by step (7 days → 30 days → 1 season).
6. **Trust** — small daily records become long-term trust.
7. **Legacy** — don't let knowledge die with the farmer.
8. **Your Part** — "this only works if you do your part" (honest input → strong output).
9. **Begin** — one minute today, stronger farming tomorrow.

**Core promise:** *Your farm runs on your effort. Your records should too.*

**The value ladder** (great for a 3-card ad sequence):
- **In 7 days** → your day starts making sense; done vs pending stops mixing up.
- **In 30 days** → spending patterns become visible; repeated mistakes become obvious.
- **In 1 season** → your farm has its own history; next decisions feel less like guesswork.

## 5. Full feature inventory

Organized by product pillar. Each pillar is a marketing angle in its own right.

### Pillar A — Voice-first daily logging (the hero)
- **One-minute voice log.** Farmer speaks naturally; the app produces a structured daily record.
- **Native spoken Marathi** (plus Hindi / English / auto-detect). Not textbook Marathi — the dialect a farmer in Pune/Nashik/Sangli actually speaks.
- **Live captions while speaking** — the farmer sees their words appear in real time (builds trust that "the machine is listening").
- **Auto-sorting AI.** One rambling sentence is split into 7 buckets: **Work · Plot · Labour · Cost · Time · Pending · Observation.**
- **Manual entry fallback** — for anyone who prefers tapping, every voice field is also editable by hand.
- **"Saved to Ledger" confirmation** — a satisfying, visual receipt of exactly what got captured, per plot, per category.

### Pillar B — Clarity & money
- **Running cost** — Today / Yesterday / Running (season-to-date) at a glance.
- **Finance Manager** — full ledger, price book, review inbox, reports, settings.
- **Cost allocation** across plots (equal / by acreage / custom) and duplicate-spend detection.
- **Income tracking** — harvest income capture per crop.
- **Service proof / reports export** — defensible records you can show a buyer, lender, or FPO.

### Pillar C — Run the farm like a system
- **Attention board** — the farm's "what needs me today," ranked Critical / Needs Attention / Watch.
- **Scheduler** — plan and assign tasks; "Close Day" ritual with done-vs-planned and daily-closure %.
- **Reflect** — an activity heatmap and day-review to see the farm's rhythm over time.
- **Compare** — stage-aware plot-vs-plot comparison (which plot is ahead, which is lagging).
- **Procurement** — track inputs/purchases.
- **Crop & plot model** — multiple crops, multiple plots, per-plot context.

### Pillar D — People, labour & trust
- **Multi-operator farms** — owner + family + mukadam, each logging under their own identity.
- **Work / Job Cards** — assign work, confirm payouts, track who did what.
- **Worker reliability score** — a reputation layer for labour.
- **Verification spine** — entries move through trust states so "everyone sees the same picture."
- **Farm sharing via QR** — invite someone to your farm by scanning a code.

### Pillar E — Weather & context
- **Weather widget** on the home screen (live, via Tomorrow.io).
- **Weather reaction prompts** — the app nudges the farmer to log when weather changes.

### Pillar F — Memory & legacy
- **Voice Diary** — a calendar of voice clips; the farm's spoken journal over time.
- **Farm history** — the accumulated record that becomes the farm's institutional memory.

### Pillar G — Privacy, consent & data rights (a trust differentiator, not fine print)
- **Consent screen** — explicit, farmer-controlled consent.
- **Self-serve data export** (DPDP §11) and **self-serve erasure** (DPDP §12).
- **PII review queue** — internal governance of personal data.
- **Forward-looking consent** — clips before consent are never archived (privacy by design).

### Pillar H — Works anywhere
- **Offline-first** — full logging with no signal; syncs automatically when back online.
- **Sync status indicator** + conflict resolution for shared farms.
- **Referrals** — built-in word-of-mouth loop.

## 6. Page-by-page walkthrough (Cofounder mode — through the marketer's lens)

This is the actual app, screen by screen. For each: **what it is**, then **what it sells**.

### 6.1 Permissions / Onboarding gate
**What it is:** First-run screen requesting microphone (and related) permissions before the farmer can speak.
**What it sells:** Honesty up front — "we ask for the mic because your voice is the whole point." Frame mic access as empowerment, not surveillance.

### 6.2 Login (OTP-first)
**What it is:** Phone-number login with a **+91** prefix shown by default, OTP-based (no passwords). Branded logo lockup, trust footer, farm-band illustration. Splash is a short logo-reveal animation.
**What it sells:** Zero-friction, password-free entry that a non-tech farmer trusts instantly. The brand's first impression — calm, premium, made-for-India.

### 6.3 First farm setup (Minimal Onboarding + First-Farm Wizard)
**What it is:** Genuinely-new users get a **2-field minimal onboarding** (just enough to start). A richer "First Farm Wizard" is available from the header for setting up crops and plots in detail. Existing users can also **join an existing farm via QR**.
**What it sells:** "You can start in under a minute." Low commitment to first value. The QR-join is a great demo of shareability ("scan and you're on the family farm").

### 6.4 Home / Daily Log — *the hero screen*
**What it is:** The heart of the app. From top to bottom:
- **Weather widget** (live conditions).
- **Daily Closure ring** — a circular % meter showing how "closed" today is, plus a **Close Day** button. Tasks Done / Planned. Pending-approval count.
- **Yesterday-not-closed nudge** — gentle pull to finish unfinished days.
- **Running Cost card** (black, premium) — Today / Yesterday / Running, with an inline "cost may be inaccurate — N entries unverified, verify now" prompt.
- **Crop/Plot selector** — pick the plot you're logging for.
- **Voice recorder** with **live captions**, or a manual-entry toggle.
- **Today's Timeline / Activity Feed** — each log as a card: work done, plot, crop, who logged it, time, verification status, and per-bucket counts.
- **"Saved to Ledger"** success state — a celebratory, per-plot breakdown of what was captured.

**What it sells:** This single screen *is* the product. The "speak → sorted → saved to ledger" loop is the money shot for every demo video, hero GIF, and app-store screenshot. The closure ring and running-cost card sell **control**. The activity feed sells **clarity**. Lead all top-of-funnel creative with this screen in motion.

### 6.5 Reflect (Home → Reflect tab)
**What it is:** An **activity heatmap** and day-review across history. Review past days, edit logs, see tasks, verify entries.
**What it sells:** "Your farm now has a memory." The heatmap is a beautiful, shareable visual of accumulated discipline — perfect for the "30-day return" story.

### 6.6 Compare (Home → Compare tab)
**What it is:** **Stage-aware plot-vs-plot comparison** with fuzzy matching — compare two plots at the same crop stage.
**What it sells:** "Which plot is winning?" Turns a farmer into an analyst. Strong for the progressive-farmer / multi-plot persona.

### 6.7 Attention (bottom nav · left)
**What it is:** The farm's daily priority board. Cards ranked **Critical / Needs Attention / Watch**, each with a suggested action (e.g., assign a test, open review inbox). Empty state: *"सगळ्या शेती आज व्यवस्थित आहेत / All your farms are on track today."*
**What it sells:** "The app tells you what needs you today." This is the proactive, intelligent face of the product — it doesn't just store, it *prompts*. Great for the "less confusion, more grip" message.

### 6.8 Procurement (bottom nav)
**What it is:** Track inputs and purchases against crops/plots.
**What it sells:** "Know where the money actually went." Feeds the spending-clarity narrative.

### 6.9 Schedule (bottom nav · center, the big button)
**What it is:** The planning surface — create and assign tasks, manage resources, and run the **Close Day** ritual.
**What it sells:** "Plan the day, then close it." The center-stage button signals this is a daily anchor. Sells routine and discipline.

### 6.10 Income (bottom nav)
**What it is:** Capture harvest income per crop/plot.
**What it sells:** The other half of the P&L. Pairs with Running Cost to complete the "now I can see profit" story.

### 6.11 Tests (bottom nav)
**What it is:** A crop-test queue (soil/health checks) with due items, detail pages, and deep-links from Attention cards.
**What it sells:** "Your farm has a checklist of what to verify." Positions ShramSafal as a serious operating system, not a notepad. (More relevant for advanced/FPO segments — keep lighter in mass-market creative.)

### 6.12 Finance Manager (and its sub-pages)
**What it is:** A full finance workspace reachable from Profile:
- **Finance Manager Home** — the money dashboard.
- **Ledger** — every entry.
- **Price Book** — standard rates for inputs/labour.
- **Review Inbox** — verify unconfirmed entries (so costs stay trustworthy).
- **Reports** — summaries and exports.
- **Service Proof** — exportable proof of work/spend.
- **Finance Settings** — defaults (e.g., labour rates).
**What it sells:** "This is real accounting, in your voice." For lenders, buyers, and FPOs, this is the credibility layer. Sell it as *defensible records*.

### 6.13 Work / Job Cards
**What it is:** **Job Cards** (assign work), **Job Card Detail** (payout confirmation, eligibility), and **Worker Profile** with a **reliability score**.
**What it sells:** "Manage labour like a pro." The worker reliability score is a genuinely novel angle — a trust/reputation layer for farm labour. Strong PR/story hook.

### 6.14 Voice Diary
**What it is:** A **calendar with dots** marking days you have clips, a **clip player**, and a **retention banner** (privacy-aware). The farm's spoken journal.
**What it sells:** Emotional, human, ownable. "Your voice, your farm's story, kept." Beautiful for brand-film and legacy-themed creative.

### 6.15 Profile
**What it is:** The farmer's identity hub — profile, crops, **people/operators**, schedule library, and entry points to Finance Manager, Referrals, and QR demo.
**What it sells:** "This is *your* farm, *your* team." Multi-operator and QR-sharing live here — the social/family dimension.

### 6.16 Settings
**What it is:** App preferences, language, and links to Consent, Voice Diary, and admin tools.
**What it sells:** Control and transparency. The bilingual toggle lives here — reinforce "the app speaks your language."

### 6.17 Consent & Data Rights (Consent · Erasure · Export · PII Review)
**What it is:** Explicit consent management, plus **self-serve data export (DPDP §11)** and **erasure (DPDP §12)**, with an internal PII review queue.
**What it sells:** **Trust as a feature.** Most agri-apps bury this; we surface it. "Your data is yours — export it or delete it, anytime, yourself." Use this to disarm the privacy objection and to win institutional/FPO trust.

### 6.18 Referrals & QR
**What it is:** A referral surface and a QR-based farm-join demo.
**What it sells:** Built-in virality. Frame referrals around trust ("tell a farmer you trust").

### 6.19 Supporting surfaces (good to know, low creative priority)
- **Sync status indicator + drawer** — visible proof that offline work is safely syncing.
- **Offline conflict resolution** — graceful handling when two people edit a shared farm.
- **Weather reaction prompt** — contextual nudge to log around weather events.
- **Compliance Signals / admin ops / AI ops** — internal/advanced governance dashboards.

## 7. USP — why ShramSafal wins (the moat)

Rank these for any positioning deck. The top three are the durable differentiators.

1. **Voice-first in the farmer's own spoken dialect.** No typing, no forms, no literacy barrier. The competitor benchmark is "fill a form" — ours is "just talk." This is the category-defining wedge.
2. **It sorts for you.** The AI doesn't just transcribe — it *separates* one spoken minute into work, cost, labour, water, inputs, machinery, and pending. That cognitive offload is the felt magic.
3. **Offline-first, built for the field.** Full functionality with no signal; auto-sync later. Most "agri-tech" silently assumes connectivity the farm doesn't have.
4. **A whole farm operating system, not a single trick.** Logging + money + scheduling + attention + labour + weather + memory in one calm app.
5. **Trust spine.** Verification states, audit trail, and multi-operator identity mean owner, family, and mukadam all see the same picture — fewer disputes.
6. **Privacy & DPDP compliance as a product surface.** Self-serve export/erasure and consent are visible features, not buried legalese — a real trust advantage with farmers and institutions.
7. **Legacy preservation.** The farm's knowledge stops dying with one person. No competitor sells this emotional benefit.
8. **Bilingual, India-first, dignity-first design.** Premium, calm UI that respects the farmer as an operator — not a charity case.

**One-sentence USP for the agency:** *ShramSafal is the only farm app where you just speak — in your own language, even offline — and it quietly turns your day into clear, trustworthy records you actually own.*

## 8. Reliability & trust story (the credibility layer)

When the agency needs to answer "is this real / can we trust it," here is the substance:

- **Already live in production.** Shipped, versioned, and running — Android APK + mobile web. Not a prototype.
- **Offline-first architecture.** Local-first storage with background sync, idempotent operations, and conflict resolution for shared farms. Work is never lost to a dead signal.
- **Bank-grade data isolation.** Postgres with row-level tenant isolation — one farm can never see another's data, enforced at the database layer.
- **Append-only audit trail.** Every entry's history is preserved; records are defensible.
- **Verification (trust) states.** Entries are marked verified/unverified so cost and work numbers can be trusted — and the app flags when they aren't yet.
- **India data residency.** Hosted in AWS Mumbai (ap-south-1) — data stays in India.
- **DPDP-aligned by design.** Consent management, self-serve export and erasure, PII governance, and privacy-by-default (pre-consent clips are never stored).
- **Disciplined engineering.** Clean architecture, signed commits, gated CI, and a 7-gate production deploy process with rollback floors. (Translate for press as: "built carefully, deployed safely.")
- **Reliable AI pipeline.** Best-in-class speech and language models for Indian languages, with a strict structured-output contract so the record is always well-formed.

**How to phrase reliability to farmers (not engineers):**
> *"Works even when your phone has no signal. Your farm's data is yours, stays in India, and only you control it. Nothing gets lost."*

## 10. Brand & voice guidelines (must-follow)

**Product name:** **ShramSafal** (श्रमसफल). The company/platform is **AgriSync** — lead with ShramSafal in all farmer-facing work.

**Tagline (canonical):** *Your farm runs on your effort. Your records should too.*
**Sticky CTA:** *1 minute. That's it.*

**Tone:**
- **English:** direct, blunt, empathetic — *not* poetic. Say it the way one farmer says it to another. Short sentences. No filler. No jargon.
- **Marathi:** बोलीभाषा (spoken Marathi), as a farmer in Pune/Nashik/Sangli actually talks. **Never** word-for-word translate the English — rewrite it natively. Marathi is a first-class language here, not a localization afterthought.

**Dignity rule:** the farmer is a competent operator/CEO of their farm. No pity, no "poor farmer" framing, no condescension. Aspirational but grounded ("built for real farm life, not for show").

**Typography (hard rules — give these to designers):**
- Marathi body text → **Noto Sans Devanagari**
- Marathi decorative/headings → **Noto Serif Devanagari**
- English / brand / numbers → **DM Sans** (and DM Serif Display for display)
- **Never** use system-ui, Arial, or generic fallbacks for visible text. In SVGs, set `font-family` explicitly.

**Visual register:** calm, premium, earthy. Emerald-green primary, warm stone/cream neutrals, soil-and-harvest motifs. Material-style clarity, not flashy gradients. Real farm life, real light.

## 13. Do's & don'ts for the agency

**Do**
- Lead with the **voice → sorted → saved** moment in motion.
- Treat **Marathi as primary**, written natively.
- Show the farmer as a **capable operator**.
- Use the real palette and the typography rules above.
- Anchor benefits in the **value ladder** (7 / 30 / 1-season).
- Sell **clarity, control, trust, legacy** — feelings, not features.

**Don't**
- Don't call it an "agri-tech platform" or "AI tool" in farmer-facing copy — it's a *daily farm work companion*.
- Don't word-for-word translate English into Marathi.
- Don't use pity, charity, or "backward farmer" framing.
- Don't over-index on advanced surfaces (Tests, admin, finance reports) in mass-market creative — keep the hero loop front and center.
- Don't use generic stock "smiling farmer with tablet" imagery — it reads fake. Real hands, real field, real phone.
- Don't promise features as "coming soon" without checking with the founder's office first.
