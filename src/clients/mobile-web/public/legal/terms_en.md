## 4. What you put into the app, and what you are responsible for

You keep ownership of everything you record. You are responsible for it being lawful and
for it being yours to record.

**This includes other people.** When you speak or type a day's work, the app reads out and
stores the **names of the workers and mukadams you mention**, along with which farm they
worked on, what was agreed and what was paid. Those people did not agree to anything on
this screen — you did.

So, in plain words: **you are telling us their names.** By using Shram Safal you confirm
that you are entitled to do so, and you agree to **tell the people who work for you that
their names and work are recorded in this app.** We ask every farmer this face to face
during onboarding. Please actually do it.

You may not enter anyone's Aadhaar number, bank details, health information or any other
sensitive identifier about a third party. The app does not need them and does not ask for
them.

## 5. What the app does — and what it does not promise

- Shram Safal turns what you say into a **record of your day**: the work, the labour, the
  inputs and the cost.
- **The app does not invent figures.** Every quantity, rate and wage in your records is
  there because you said it or typed it. Where the app is unsure, it asks.
- Anything the app works out for you — a total, a comparison, a suggestion — is a
  **working aid, not advice**. It is not agronomic advice, not financial advice, not legal
  advice and not a wage or tax computation you should file on. Check it before you rely on
  it.
- Weather comes from an outside supplier and is a forecast. It will sometimes be wrong.
- Speech understanding is imperfect, particularly in noise. Read what the app understood
  before you confirm it. A record you have not confirmed is shown to you as unconfirmed,
  and that is the honest state, not an error.
